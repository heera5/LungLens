Common Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

If you regularly experience one or more of these, taking the assessment can help you decide when to see a specialist.

A Cough That Won't Go Away

A persistent cough lasting longer than 3 to 4 weeks, especially if dry, hacking, or producing thick phlegm.

Feeling Short of Breath

Feeling unusually winded after climbing stairs, walking short distances, or even while sitting quietly.

Wheezing or Whistling Sounds

A whistling, squeaking, or rattling sound when you exhale, caused by inflammation or narrowing of the airways.

Chest Tightness or Discomfort

An ongoing dull ache or tightness in the chest that is more noticeable when you take a deep breath or laugh.

Smoking & Nicotine Signs

Regular smoking exposure or nicotine staining on fingertips, which greatly increases long-term lung vulnerability.

Unexplained Fatigue & Trouble Swallowing

Deep exhaustion that sleep doesn't resolve, sometimes with discomfort when swallowing food.

Everyday Ways to Protect Your Lungs

Practical steps recommended by respiratory therapists and doctors to keep your airways healthy.

Quit Smoking or Stay Smoke-Free

Within 2 to 3 months of quitting, lung function improves significantly and chronic coughing begins to decline.

Avoid Secondhand Smoke & Toxins

Keep your home and vehicle smoke-free, test for radon, and wear a mask around dust or chemicals.

Stay Physically Active

Regular moderate exercise like brisk walking or swimming helps your lungs move oxygen more efficiently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this assessment a replacement for a doctor?

No. LungLens is an educational self-assessment tool. It helps you organize symptoms and identify risk factors so you can have a more informed conversation with your doctor. Only a licensed physician can diagnose a medical condition.

Will my answers be kept private?

Yes. Your responses are evaluated during your session and are not sold or shared with third parties. CT images are processed in memory and never stored.

What if my results show a higher risk?

Don't panic. A higher risk rating means your answers or scan match patterns seen in respiratory conditions. Print the summary and see your doctor or a pulmonologist for a clinical checkup.
